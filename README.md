# ADE Human Machine Framework

## Human First. Context Appropriate.

The ADE Human Machine Framework is an open standards initiative exploring how information can be communicated between humans and machines without making the screen the required center of the experience.

The central idea is simple:

> Information should adapt to the human, not require the human to adapt to the information system.

Today, much of the digital world is designed around screens.

A person receives information through a phone, computer, dashboard, display, or other visual interface. Other ways of receiving information are often added later to support accessibility, convenience, or specific situations.

ADE explores a different approach.

The information itself should be separated from the way it is experienced.

---

# The ADE Approach

ADE explores an information architecture in which meaning is separated from presentation.

The basic model is:

```text
INFORMATION
     +
RECEIVER
     +
INTENT
     +
CONTEXT
     +
CAPABILITY
     ↓
ADE DECISION
     ↓
EXPERIENCE REQUIREMENTS
     ↓
APPROPRIATE EXPERIENCE
```

The same information may therefore be experienced differently depending on:

* Who is receiving it
* What they are trying to do
* Where they are
* What is happening around them
* What they can perceive
* What devices are available
* How important the information is
* How much attention is available

The underlying meaning does not need to change.

The experience can change.

---

# One Information Source. Many Possible Experiences.

Consider a severe weather warning:

```text
EVENT
Severe weather

STATE
Approaching

LOCATION
Affected area

SIGNIFICANCE
Critical

REQUIREMENT
Seek shelter

TIME
Immediate
```

The receiving system may determine that the appropriate experience is:

* Spoken information for a driver
* Haptic notification for a person walking
* Visual information for someone looking at a display
* A combination of audio and visual information
* A translated experience
* A simplified experience for a child
* A detailed experience for someone who needs more information
* Machine readable information for another system

The information remains the same.

The experience changes according to the situation.

---

# Why ADE Exists

People do not experience the world through screens alone.

People see, hear, touch, move, speak, observe, interact, and respond to their surroundings.

Yet much of today's digital information architecture assumes that a person will look at a screen.

This can create:

* Unnecessary distraction
* Reduced awareness of surroundings
* Increased cognitive effort
* Information overload
* Barriers for people with different abilities
* Communication difficulties across languages and cultures
* Poor interaction while driving or operating equipment
* Less natural interaction with technology
* Greater dependence on personal devices

ADE explores whether information can instead be designed around the human and the situation in which the information is received.

---

# ADE Is Not a Screen Replacement

ADE is not an attempt to remove screens.

Screens are useful and will remain an important way to experience information.

The goal is to remove the assumption that a screen must be the primary or required experience.

A screen may be the best choice in one situation.

Audio may be better in another.

Haptic information may be better in another.

A combination of methods may be best in another.

The architecture should allow the receiving system to make that decision.

---

# ADE Is Not an Accessibility Standard

Accessibility is an important part of ADE.

However, ADE is not intended to be limited to disability access.

The same architecture could benefit:

* People with disabilities
* Children
* Older adults
* Drivers
* Workers
* People operating machinery
* People with their hands occupied
* People in noisy environments
* People in quiet environments
* People learning
* People communicating across languages
* People using different devices
* People simply trying to remain present in the physical world

Accessibility is one important application of human first information architecture.

It is not the entire purpose.

# The ADE Standards Architecture

ADE is intended to be a collection of related standards and frameworks rather than a single specification.

The repository provides a common place for developing and researching those standards.

The architecture is expected to evolve.

Potential areas include:

```text
ADE
│
└── ADE Human Machine Framework
    │
    ├── ADE USLF
    ├── ADE CDT
    ├── ADE IF
    ├── ADE LF
    ├── ADE HTF
    └── ADE HMF
```

These names represent areas of ADE development and research.

Their relationships, boundaries, and final specifications remain subject to research.

---

# Human Machine Framework

ADE HMF explores the architectural relationship between:

```text
Information
Human
Intent
Context
Capability
Experience
```

The current hypothesis is:

> Information describes what matters. Context helps determine how it should be experienced.

A second working principle is:

> Shared Meaning, Independent Experience.

These are ADE architectural propositions.

They are not presented as established industry standards.

---
